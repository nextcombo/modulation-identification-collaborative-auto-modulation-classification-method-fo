# [Modulation Identification] Collaborative Auto-modulation Classification Method for MIMO Systems Based on Deep Learning [with MATLAB + Python Code]

Automatic Modulation Classification (AMC) is one of the most critical algorithms for identifying the modulation types of non-cooperative communication systems. In recent years, studies have shown that AMC methods based on deep learning (DL) are effective in single input single output (SISO) systems but have been less explored in multi-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems. This paper proposes a Cooperative AMC (Co-AMC) method based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for MIMO systems, where a receiver equipped with multiple antennas collaborates to identify the modulation type. Specifically, each receiving antenna provides sub-identification results through CNN, and then the decision-maker recognizes the modulation type based on these sub-results and collaborative decision rules such as Direct Voting (DV), Weighted Voting (WV), Direct Average (DA), and Weighted Average (WA). Simulation results show that among the four collaborative decision rules, the Co-AMC method based on CNN and WA has the highest correct classification probability. Moreover, the Co-AMC method based on CNN also outperforms traditional AMC methods based on Higher Order Cumulants (HOC), reflecting the effective feature extraction capability and strong classification ability of CNN.
Introduction: Automatic Modulation Classification (AMC) is one of the most critical technologies in non-cooperative communication systems [1]-[5] and cognitive radio (CR) assisted systems [6]-[8]. AMC can enhance the cognitive capabilities of communication systems by identifying the modulation types of unknown signals, widely applied in military and civilian fields [9]. For SISO systems [9]-[14] and MIMO systems [15]-[17], various AMC methods have been proposed. These AMC methods can be divided into two categories, based on decision theory and pattern recognition [15]. Decision theory-based AMC methods rely on perfect channel state information (CSI) and precise noise variance [18].
However, AMC methods based on pattern recognition can break free from the constraints of prior information. These AMC methods can be divided into two sub-methods: feature extraction and classification. Among these methods, common artificial features include higher order statistics (HOS), cyclic features, etc. Higher order statistics are one of the most widely used features in SISO and MIMO systems due to their effectiveness in suppressing additive Gaussian white noise (AWGN) with an order o≥3 [14]-[17]. Additionally, Support Vector Machines (SVM), Decision Trees (DT), and traditional Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) are commonly used as classifiers [15]-[17].
In recent years, due to the powerful classification and prediction capabilities of deep learning (DL) [27]-[34], it has been combined with various communication technologies such as network traffic control [19]-[22], non-orthogonal multiple access [23]-[25], fast beamforming [26], and AMC [9]. However, deep learning-based AMC methods are mainly designed for SISO systems. In [9], T. O'Shea and J. Hoydis first proposed an AMC method based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for SISO systems, which outperformed traditional AMC methods significantly. B. Tang et al. proposed a novel semi-supervised AMC method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) and constellation diagrams for point-to-point communication using single antennas in [10]. Moreover, S. Hu et al. proposed an AMC method based on stacked Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) for wireless communication systems with non-Gaussian noise, which outperformed some previously proposed AMC methods.
The introduction of deep neural networks such as CNN or LSTM into MIMO systems for AMC research is relatively scarce. This paper differs from the AMC methods based on HOC and ANN in [15] and [16], proposing a Cooperative AMC (Co-AMC) method for MIMO systems by using CNN and collaborative decision rules. Specifically, CNN is trained on datasets with all receiving antennas, followed by providing identification sub-results for each receiving antenna. Decision makers combine these identification sub-results to recognize modulation types through collaborative decision rules. Simulation results show that the performance of the Co-AMC method based on CNN outperforms the AMC methods based on HOC and ANN. Additionally, we compared different collaborative decision rules including direct voting (DV), weighted voting (WV), direct average (DA), and weighted average (WA), where the Co-AMC method based on WA demonstrated the best classification performance among the four collaborative decision rules.
Figure: In conclusion, this paper proposes a Cooperative AMC method for MIMO systems based on CNN. Four collaborative decision rules (DV, WV, DA, and WA methods) are used for multiple receiving antennas to collaboratively determine the final modulation type. Simulation results indicate that in the Co-AMC method based on CNN, the performance of the voting method is weaker than that of the average method. Moreover, the weighted method (where weights are determined by the verification accuracy of each antenna) outperforms the direct method, which assigns equal weights to each antenna. Furthermore, under the same collaborative rule, the proposed Co-AMC method based on CNN outperforms traditional AMC methods based on HOC and ANN. The results suggest that compared to traditional manual feature design methods, CNN can extract more effective modulation classification features.
